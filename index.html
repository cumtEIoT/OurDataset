Abstract

Simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) in global navigation satellite system (GNSS)-denied environments remains challenging because of sensing degradation, geometric degeneracy, heterogeneous platforms, and limited computational resources. This paper presents DECODE, a cross-platform multi-sensor SLAM dataset for GNSS-denied, constrained, and degenerate environments. DECODE contains 60 sequences collected using handheld, unmanned ground vehicle (UGV), and drone platforms in several representative scenarios, including the indoor room, underground garage, long corridor, metro tunnel, and mine tunnel.

The sensing configurations integrate RGB-D and fisheye cameras, heterogeneous LiDAR sensors with spinning, optionally repetitive, and non-repetitive scanning patterns. To validate the utility of DECODE in advancing SLAM research, we evaluate state-of-the-art SLAM methods including visual-inertial, LiDAR-inertial, and LiDAR-inertial-visual solutions in terms of absolute trajectory error (ATE), relative trajectory error (RTE), and processing latency on both a high-performance server and an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin platform.

DECODE therefore provides a unified and challenging benchmark for robust SLAM, heterogeneous sensor fusion, cross-platform evaluation, and resource-constrained robotic deployment in complex underground environments. The complete dataset, including the ground-truth, calibration files, and sensor data, will be available at https://cumteiot.github.io/DECODE/.

System Overview

DECODE is collected using three mobile platforms — a handheld device, an unmanned ground vehicle (UGV), and a drone — to support cross-platform multi-sensor data collection in GNSS-denied environments. Each platform is equipped with heterogeneous LiDAR sensors, RGB-D cameras, and built-in IMUs according to its motion characteristics, load capacity, and application requirements, targeting constrained scenarios such as indoor rooms, underground garages, corridors, metro tunnels, and mine tunnels.

The three data-collection platforms used in DECODE: handheld, UGV, and drone.

The handheld platform is deployed across all scenarios and provides flexible, human-carried motion with complex trajectories and frequent viewpoint changes. It carries an Intel RealSense D455 RGB-D camera (global shutter, 1280×720, 30 Hz) together with two heterogeneous LiDAR sensors — LIVOX AVIA and LIVOX MID360 — and their built-in BMI088 / BMI055 IMUs (200 Hz).

The UGV platform benefits from higher payload capacity and smooth, stable motion. It is equipped with the RealSense D455, three LiDAR sensors (LIVOX AVIA, LIVOX MID360, and Ouster OS0-gen2-128, a 128-line spinning LiDAR with an InvenSense ICM-20948 IMU), enabling high-resolution geometric sensing and fine mapping.

The drone platform provides aerial viewpoints and three-dimensional motion patterns. It carries a lightweight LIVOX MID360, an Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D camera (rolling shutter, 640×480, 15 Hz), and an Intel RealSense T265 fisheye camera (global shutter, 848×800, 15 Hz) for wide-FOV visual observation under rapid motion.

Sensor Clock Synchronization

Within each device, LiDAR and inertial measurements are intrinsically time-correlated. For cross-modal synchronization between cameras and LiDAR, DECODE intentionally preserves the native asynchronous acquisition characteristics without hardware-level triggering or a shared clock. This design better reflects practical robotic deployments and supports research on asynchronous visual-LiDAR fusion, inter-sensor time-offset estimation, and robust cross-modal alignment.

Calibration

Camera intrinsics are estimated with the Kalibr toolbox. LiDAR-camera extrinsics are calibrated with FAST-Calib, LiDAR-IMU extrinsics with LI-Init, and camera-IMU extrinsics with Kalibr, providing accurate rigid-body transformations for multi-sensor fusion.

Representative camera-IMU calibration residuals and estimated biases for the handheld platform.

Ground Truth

To accommodate different scene scales and deployment conditions, DECODE uses two complementary ground-truth systems. A NOKOV Mars 1.3H motion-capture system (240 Hz, 0.2 mm accuracy) provides reference trajectories in bounded indoor scenes, while a SOUTH NTS-332R10 total station (up to 1000 m range, ~5 mm ranging error) is used for large-scale environments such as long corridors, underground garages, metro tunnels, and mine tunnels.

Ground-truth systems used in DECODE: NOKOV Mars 1.3H motion capture and SOUTH NTS-332R10 total station.

Dataset

The DECODE dataset includes three essential attributes:

  • Cross-platform: data are collected on handheld, UGV, and drone platforms, enabling the analysis of algorithm adaptability across different motion modes, mounting configurations, and application scenarios.
  • Multi-sensor: RGB-D and fisheye cameras, heterogeneous LiDAR sensors (spinning, optionally-repetitive, and non-repetitive), and built-in IMUs provide a unified testing foundation for visual, LiDAR, inertial, and fusion methods.
  • GNSS-denied & degenerate scenarios: underground tunnels, semi-enclosed spaces, and other constrained environments highlight challenges such as weak texture, low illumination, dynamic occlusion, dust, and geometric degradation.

DECODE contains a total of 60 data sequences, with an aggregate recording duration of approximately 3.8 hours and a cumulative trajectory length of approximately 3.3 km. Among them, 25 sequences are collected using the handheld platform, 18 using the UGV platform, and 17 using the drone platform, spanning five scenarios: the indoor room (10), underground garage (14), long corridor (15), metro tunnel (6), and mine tunnel (15).

Scenarios

Illustration of diverse scenarios with challenges such as low illumination, weak texture, and repetitive structure.

Indoor Room

The indoor room sequences represent relatively enclosed environments, spanning both open laboratory-like spaces and rooms containing obstacles, equipment, and partitions. They provide baseline conditions for trajectory estimation, sensor fusion, and map reconstruction, and are useful for evaluating robustness to local occlusion, viewpoint changes, and constrained motion.

Underground Garage

The underground garage sequences feature larger spatial scales, longer trajectories, and complex structural layouts with regularly arranged columns, parking lanes, walls, and vehicles. Repetitive structures produce perceptual aliasing, while uneven illumination and dynamic vehicles introduce occlusion, making these sequences suitable for evaluating long-term drift and robustness to repetitive structures.

Long Corridor

The long-corridor sequences represent one of the most typical degenerate environments in DECODE, with elongated layouts, parallel walls, flat floors, repetitive doors, and very weak illumination. For visual methods, weak textures reduce feature reliability, while for LiDAR methods the parallel surfaces provide insufficient geometric constraints along the corridor axis, making these sequences ideal for evaluating cumulative drift and degeneracy handling.

Metro Tunnel

The metro tunnel sequences represent large-scale linear underground infrastructure with railway tracks, curved linings, and limited illumination. Strong longitudinal repetition leads to insufficient pose constraints along the tunnel direction, imposing stringent requirements on long-distance localization, degeneracy-aware estimation, and loop-closure detection.

Mine Tunnel

The mine tunnel sequences contain narrow passages, rough wall surfaces, irregular cross-sections, temporary equipment, and airborne dust. The simultaneous presence of visual degradation, geometric degeneracy, dust interference, and dynamic disturbances makes these sequences particularly valuable for evaluating the robustness and environmental adaptability of multi-sensor SLAM systems under realistic industrial conditions.

Data format

All sensor measurements are recorded using the Robot Operating System (ROS). Each ROS bag contains the original sensor topics of the corresponding platform, including LiDAR point clouds, inertial measurements, RGB images, depth images, and fisheye images. The dataset is organized into three main directories: calibration_files, groundtruth, and sensor_data. Each ground-truth trajectory is stored as a text file, while the raw sensor data is stored as a ROS bag file.

Online Benchmark

  1. The DECODE benchmark evaluates submitted SLAM methods on all 60 sequences using the EVO toolkit, reporting both absolute trajectory error (ATE) and relative trajectory error (RTE) as root mean square error (RMSE).
  2. We provide author-tested results for six representative methods — VINS-Fusion, FAST-LIO2, R³LIVE, SR-LIVO, Ground-Fusion++, and FAST-LIVO2 — on both a high-performance server and an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin embedded platform.
  3. Ground-truth trajectories remain hidden for future benchmark submissions to ensure a fair and reproducible comparison.
